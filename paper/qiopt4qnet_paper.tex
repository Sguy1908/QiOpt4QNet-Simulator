\begin{abstract}
We formulate entanglement routing in quantum repeater networks---the
joint selection of a path and purification schedule (a \emph{bundle})
per concurrent request under edge and memory capacity
constraints---as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO)
problem, and compare a Metropolis annealer, a boundary--matrix-product-state
(MPS) tensor-network compressor, OpenJij simulated annealing, and an
embedded quantum-annealing backend against an exact-ILP reference.  On
chain, grid, and five generative topology families the tensor-network
solver meets or exceeds the Metropolis served ratio (utilities agree
to within $\sim\!1\%$ wherever the two return identical selections),
while the MPS compressor is $5$--$4{,}000\times$ faster and the
exact-ILP benchmark certifies a mean relative optimality gap of $1.5\%$
for the Metropolis solver.  We additionally derive a request-conditioned
reachable-load calibration for the edge and memory capacity penalties
that preserves exact ground-state correspondence while never exceeding
the utility-scale reference bound.  It yields substantially smaller
coefficients on instances whose reachable-load structure permits
tightening, with no detectable systematic change in feasibility,
overload, or repaired solution quality under matched fixed-budget SA
and SQA.  Beyond the static formulation we develop a
stochastic reliability framework in which a discrete-event simulator
executes solver plans under sampled entanglement generation, swap
failure, T1/T2 decoherence, and fidelity noise.  Within it we (i)
introduce \emph{chance-constrained routing}, replacing the hard rule
$F \ge F_{\min}$ with the quantile constraint
$\Pr[F_r \ge F_{\min}] \ge 1-\varepsilon$, and show that the
deterministic rule implicitly tolerates $5$--$14\%$ SLA violation for
razor-margin bundles while the chance rule keeps the empirical
violation at or below the requested $\varepsilon$ on every tested
instance; (ii) benchmark recourse, in which local repair recovers
$100\%$ of failed requests at a $334$--$1065\times$ wall-time speedup
over full replanning; and (iii) add purification as a first-class
variable, showing that a purification-aware candidate superset is
cost-free while purification binds precisely when fidelity
requirements outrun raw-link fidelity.  The full stack---temporal-memory
joint scheduling, adaptive and GNN-guided candidate reduction, a hybrid
reduce--solve--repair pipeline, multi-objective Pareto analysis,
$k$-disjoint-path provisioning, swap-tree ordering under memory decay,
and hardware-parameter profiles---is implemented, unit-tested, and
reproducible, and delivers every admitted request within coherence at
mean delivered fidelity $0.30$--$0.51$ where a memory-agnostic baseline
over-admits. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantum networks distribute entanglement between distant nodes, the
resource behind quantum key distribution
(QKD)~\cite{ekert1991quantum,gisin2002quantum,scarani2009security,
pirandola2020advances}, distributed quantum
computing, and blind quantum
computing~\cite{kimble2008quantum,wehner2018quantum,simon2017towards}.
The central resource-allocation problem is: given concurrent
entanglement requests between node pairs, select for each request a
path and a purification schedule---collectively a \emph{bundle}---that
maximizes end-to-end fidelity while respecting edge (Bell-pair) and
node (memory-qubit) capacities \cite{briegel1998quantum,
dur1999entanglement,sangouard2011quantum,azuma2023quantum}.

This is a constrained combinatorial optimization problem with
structure unique to quantum systems: entanglement generation and
swapping are probabilistic \cite{bennett1993teleporting,
vanmeter2014quantum}; stored Bell pairs decay under T1/T2
decoherence; and the end-to-end fidelity of a multi-hop connection
depends jointly on elementary-link quality, purification
rounds~\cite{bennett1996purification}, and the order in which swaps
are performed.  A substantial literature addresses routing with
heuristic path selection~\cite{vanmeter2014quantum}, integer and
linear programming~\cite{pant2022routing,caleffi2017optimal,
gyongyosi2019entanglement}, percolation and
coding~\cite{cuquet2009entanglement,schoute2016shortcuts,
kozlowski2020designing}, and reinforcement
learning~\cite{li2025qlearning,boyan1993packet,mao2017routing,
rusek2020routenets}, and dedicated simulators such as NetSquid and
SeQUeNCe~\cite{coopmans2021netsquid,wu2021sequence} model the physics
faithfully.  By contrast, few works cast the \emph{full
request-allocation} problem as a single energy-minimization
(QUBO/Ising) problem over one binary decision vector; the closest
prior work is the QKD routing model of
Rosales~\cite{rosales2025quantum}, which we build upon directly.

The contributions of this paper are organized around a core QUBO
formulation and four extensions toward physical realism:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Core QUBO formulation and solver families.}  We
    formalize entanglement routing as a QUBO/Ising energy
    minimization (Sec.~\ref{sec:qubo}), introduce a direct
    slack-free Hamiltonian
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:direct}), and benchmark Metropolis, boundary-MPS,
    OpenJij simulated annealing, and an embedded quantum-annealing
    backend against an exact-ILP reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-core}).
    We additionally derive a request-conditioned reachable-load
    calibration of the edge and memory capacity penalties that preserves
    exact ground-state correspondence while never exceeding the
    utility-scale reference bound (Sec.~\ref{sec:penalty-calibration}),
    and evaluate its tightening on 1,080 benchmark instances
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-penalty}).
  \item \textbf{Stochastic reliability framework.}  We execute solver
    plans in a discrete-event simulator (DES) under sampled
    generation, swap, decoherence, and fidelity noise
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:des}), and we introduce
    \emph{chance-constrained routing}---the quantile constraint
    $\Pr[F_r \ge F_{\min}] \ge 1-\varepsilon$---which we prove, in
    simulation, keeps the empirical SLA-violation probability at or
    below the requested $\varepsilon$ while the hard rule
    $F \ge F_{\min}$ implicitly tolerates $5$--$14\%$ SLA violation for
    razor-margin bundles (Sec.~\ref{sec:chance}).  We add
    \emph{recourse}---local repair of failed requests---and
    \emph{optimality certification} by exact ILP
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:recourse-opt}).
  \item \textbf{Temporal and learned extensions.}  Temporal-memory
    joint scheduling with T1/T2 decay and per-request deadlines
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}); filter-based top-$k$, hybrid
    reduce--solve--repair, and GraphSAGE-guided candidate reduction
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:adaptive-qubo}); and purification as a first-class
    variable (Sec.~\ref{sec:purification}).
  \item \textbf{Analysis extensions.}  Multi-objective Pareto and
    $\epsilon$-constraint frontiers, $k$-disjoint-path provisioning,
    swap-tree ordering under memory decay, generative topology
    families, time-dependent decoherence-aware routing, a
    Q-learning router, and hardware-parameter profiles
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:extensions}).
\end{enumerate}
All solvers, extensions, experiments, and tests are released as an
open-source, fully reproducible suite%
\footnote{\url{https://github.com/arnavk23/QiOpt4QNet-Simulator}}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Entanglement Routing in Quantum Networks}
\label{sec:related-net}

The quantum-internet vision~\cite{kimble2008quantum,wehner2018quantum,
simon2017towards} motivates routing protocols that allocate
entangled pairs across multi-hop repeater
networks~\cite{briegel1998quantum,dur1999entanglement,
sangouard2011quantum,azuma2023quantum}.  Pant et
al.~\cite{pant2022routing} formalize \emph{routing entanglement in the
quantum internet} and study elementary-link generation scheduling;
Caleffi~\cite{caleffi2017optimal} analyzes optimal routing in the
probabilistic-capacity regime; Gyongyosi and
Imre~\cite{gyongyosi2019entanglement} introduce entanglement-gradient
routing; Cuquet and Calsamiglia~\cite{cuquet2009entanglement} analyze
entanglement percolation in complex networks; Schoute et
al.~\cite{schoute2016shortcuts} and Kozlowski et
al.~\cite{kozlowski2020designing} address greedy routing and protocol
stacks for dynamic requests; and Van Meter~\cite{vanmeter2014quantum}
provides a comprehensive textbook treatment.  Network simulators such
as NetSquid~\cite{coopmans2021netsquid} and
SeQUeNCe~\cite{wu2021sequence} provide physically faithful, usually
instance-by-instance, evaluation.  Our work differs in treating the
entire request-allocation problem as a single binary optimization over
capacity-aware \emph{bundles}, evaluated end-to-end in a stochastic
event engine, rather than per-request greedy path selection.

\subsection{QUBO/Ising Optimization and Annealing}
\label{sec:related-qubo}

QUBO is the standard interface for quantum and simulated
annealing~\cite{kadowaki1998quantum,kirkpatrick1983optimization,
johnson2011quantum,boixo2014evidence}.  Lucas~\cite{lucas2014ising}
catalogs Ising encodings of NP-hard problems; Glover et
al.~\cite{glover2019tutorial} give a modern QUBO tutorial with
penalty-formulation guidance; Tanahashi et al.~\cite{tanahashi2019application}
demonstrate an Ising-based methodology on portfolio optimization;
Farhi et al.~\cite{farhi2014qaoa} propose the QAOA circuit ansatz;
and Preskill~\cite{preskill2018nisq} frames the NISQ era in which such
encodings are most relevant.  OpenJij~\cite{openjij} provides the
simulated/quantum-annealing samplers we use, and
Venegas-Andraca et al.~\cite{venegas2018quantum} survey practical
quantum annealing on real hardware.  Our quantum-annealing backend
(Sec.~\ref{sec:qa}) applies this line of work to entanglement routing
on an embedded hardware lattice.

Penalty weights must exclude infeasible optima without overwhelming
objective resolution~\cite{verma2022penalty,alessandroni2025bigm}.
Knapsack studies derive max-profit sufficient
scales~\cite{quintero2021knapsack,guney2025multiknapsack}, while
exact, change-ratio, automatic, structure-aware, and SDP-based methods
use other problem information~\cite{garcia2022exact,
ayodele2022penalty,liu2025multiple,liu2026structure,
alessandroni2025bigm}.  Routing work likewise gives a sufficient but
non-tight coefficient~\cite{seker2024routing}.  Our problem-specific
certificate combines multiple-choice structure~\cite{sinha1979multiple}
with attainable-load dynamic programming~\cite{trick2003dynamic},
bounding utility lost against each reachable removal benefit before
taking global edge and memory maxima.

\subsection{Tensor Networks for Optimization}
\label{sec:related-tn}

Matrix-product-state (MPS) methods approximate joint distributions
over binary variables and serve as classical surrogates for quantum
annealing.  Hao et al.~\cite{hao2022quantum} solve constrained
optimization with tensor networks and find MPS contraction outperforms
simulated annealing on strongly locally coupled problems;
Preisser et al.~\cite{preisser2026variational} provide a systematic
methodology for variational MPS optimization; Nakada et
al.~\cite{nakada2025quick} encode hard constraints into tensor-network
structure directly; and Mata Ali~\cite{mata2025explicit,
mata2023polynomial} gives scaling frameworks for tensor-QUBO solvers.
The underlying machinery traces to the DMRG revolution and MPS
canonicalization~\cite{white1992dmrg,schollwock2011dmrg,
vidal2003efficient,orus2014practical,orus2019tensor}.

\subsection{Learning-Based and Robust Routing}
\label{sec:related-learning}

Reinforcement learning has a long history in network routing---Boyan
and Littman~\cite{boyan1993packet} used Q-learning for dynamic packet
routing, Mao et al.~\cite{mao2017routing} review deep-learning routing,
and Rusek et al.~\cite{rusek2020routenets} apply graph neural networks
to traffic estimation.  Game-theoretic analysis of selfish routing by
Koutsoupias and Papadimitriou~\cite{koutsoupias1999worst} and
Roughgarden and Tardos~\cite{roughgarden2002bad}, building on
Braess~\cite{braess1968paradoxon}, motivates our price-of-anarchy
extensions; Deb~\cite{deb2001multiobjective} supplies the
multi-objective background for our Pareto analysis.

\subsection{Stochastic Programming and Chance Constraints}
\label{sec:related-chance}

When resources are probabilistic, deterministic constraints must be
replaced by reliability constraints.  Chance-constrained
programming~\cite{charnes1959chance,prekopa1995stochastic} enforces
inequalities of the form $\Pr[g(x,\xi) \le 0] \ge 1-\varepsilon$ under
stochastic parameters $\xi$; two-stage and multi-stage stochastic
programming~\cite{birge1997introduction} make here-and-now versus
wait-and-see decisions; and robust optimization~\cite{bertsimas2004price,
ben-tal2009robust} bounds worst-case parameter sets.  We apply the
chance-constrained paradigm to fidelity requirements in entanglement
routing (Sec.~\ref{sec:chance}): to our knowledge it is the first such
use in the routing literature, where the fidelity requirement is
traditionally enforced deterministically.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:qubo}

\subsection{Network Model and Bundles}
\label{sec:model}

We model the quantum network as a graph $G=(V,E)$ with edge
capacities $C_e$ (Bell pairs per time epoch), edge latencies
$\ell_e$, and raw elementary-link fidelities $F_e^{(0)}$; node
memory capacities $M_v^{\max}$ (qubit slots).  A request $r$ specifies
a source--destination pair $(s_r,d_r)$, a minimum fidelity
$F_{\min}^{(r)}$, and a weight $w_r$.  The bundle generator produces a
candidate set $B_r$ per request: a bundle $b$ is a path $p$ plus a
purification profile $q$ (rounds of BBPSSW purification per
link~\cite{bennett1996purification}), with utility
$u_{r,b}$, edge demands $d_{r,b}^e$, memory demands $m_{r,b}^v$,
latency $\ell_{r,b}$, end-to-end fidelity $F_{r,b}$, and success
probability $p_{\text{succ}}^{(b)}$.

Entanglement swapping succeeds with probability $p_{\text{swap}}=1/2$
and combines two Werner fidelities via
$f_1 \star f_2 = f_1 f_2 + (1-f_1)(1-f_2)/3$, the composition law for
Werner states~\cite{werner1989quantum,bennett1993teleporting,
vanmeter2014quantum}.  Each BBPSSW purification round updates link
fidelity
\begin{equation}
  F' = \frac{F^2 + (1-F)^2/3}{F^2 + 2F(1-F)/3 + 5(1-F)^2/9},
\end{equation}
whose denominator is the round's success probability.  The end-to-end
success probability of a bundle is
$p_{\text{succ}}^{(b)} = p_{\text{swap}}^{L}\prod_{e}
p_{\text{purif}}(q_e)$, where $L$ is the number of links.  The utility
of selecting bundle $b$ for request $r$ is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:utility}
  u_{r,b} = w_r \cdot p_{\text{succ}}^{(b)} \cdot
    \bigl[1 + \beta \max(0,\, F_{r,b} - F_{\min}^{(r)})\bigr]
    - \lambda_\ell\, \ell_{r,b}
    - \lambda_c\, \text{cost}_{r,b},
\end{equation}
rewarding success probability and fidelity margin while penalizing
latency and Bell-pair expenditure.

\subsection{QUBO Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:qubo-ham}

Let $x_{r,b} \in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether bundle $b$ is selected for
request $r$, and let $L_e = \sum_{r,b} d_{r,b}^e x_{r,b}$ and
$M_v = \sum_{r,b} m_{r,b}^v x_{r,b}$ be the induced edge and memory
loads.  The penalty-based QUBO is
\begin{equation}
  \calH = \calH_{\text{util}} + \calH_{\text{one}} + \calH_{\text{edge}} + \calH_{\text{mem}},
  \label{eq:qubo-ham}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
  \calH_{\text{util}} &= -\sum_{r,b} u_{r,b}\, x_{r,b},\\
  \calH_{\text{one}} &= A \sum_r
    \sum_{\substack{b,b'\in B_r\\b<b'}} x_{r,b}x_{r,b'},\\
  \calH_{\text{edge}} &= B \sum_e \bigl(L_e + s_e - C_e\bigr)^2,\\
  \calH_{\text{mem}} &= D \sum_v \bigl(M_v + s_v - M_v^{\max}\bigr)^2,
\end{align}
where $s_e,s_v$ are slack variables expanded in binary
($\mathcal{O}((|E|+|V|)\log_2 C)$ additional variables), and $A,B,D$
are penalty coefficients.

\subsection{Request-Conditioned Penalty Calibration}
\label{sec:penalty-calibration}

The calibration applies to the QUBO above with nonnegative integer
demands and capacities and zero soft-congestion weights.  Because the binary
slack expansion represents every integer in $0,\ldots,K$, minimizing over it for 
fixed $x$ turns a capacity term into $[L-K]_+^2$, where $[z]_+=\max(z,0)$.  
To simplify notation, treat $b\in B_r$ as a request-labelled bundle and 
write $u_b=u_{r,b}$, $d_{e,b}=d_{r,b}^e$, and $m_{v,b}=m_{r,b}^v$.

Let $u_b^+=[u_b]_+$,
$P_0=\max(\{u_b^+:b\}\cup\{0\})$, and
$\epsilon=\max(10^{-9},10^{-6}P_0)$.  The utility-scale reference is
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{ref}}=B_{\mathrm{ref}}=D_{\mathrm{ref}}
  =P_0+\epsilon.
  \label{eq:reference-calibration}
\end{equation}
This is the max-profit-style sufficient scale used in related
logarithmic-slack knapsack encodings~\cite{quintero2021knapsack,
guney2025multiknapsack}.

For bundle $b\in B_r$ and edge $e$, define the co-loads reachable from
the other requests by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R_{e,-r}=
  \left\{\sum_{r'\ne r}d_{e,b_{r'}}:
    b_{r'}\in B_{r'}\cup\{\varnothing\}\ \forall r'\ne r\right\},
  \label{eq:reachable-loads}
\end{equation}
where $\varnothing$ contributes zero.  If $d_{e,b}>0$ and overload is
reachable, set
\begin{align}
  \rho^*_{e,b}
    &=\min\{\rho\in\mathcal R_{e,-r}:\rho+d_{e,b}>C_e\},\\
  \delta_{e,b}
    &=[\rho^*_{e,b}+d_{e,b}-C_e]_+^2
      -[\rho^*_{e,b}-C_e]_+^2.
  \label{eq:edge-penalty-drop}
\end{align}
Undefined pairs are omitted.  The memory quantities
$\mathcal R_{v,-r}$, $\rho^*_{v,b}$, and $\delta_{v,b}$ follow by
replacing $(e,d_{e,b},C_e)$ with $(v,m_{v,b},M_v^{\max})$.
For $\rho+d>C$, the penalty decrease from removing a bundle is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(\rho)=
  \begin{cases}
    (\rho+d-C)^2, & \rho\le C,\\
    2d(\rho-C)+d^2, & \rho>C.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:drop-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
Both branches are nondecreasing, so
$\rho_1\le\rho_2\Rightarrow\Delta(\rho_1)\le\Delta(\rho_2)$.
Thus $\rho^*$ gives the smallest reachable penalty decrease.  Bounded
request-wise dynamic programming finds $\rho^*$.

The global coefficients are
\begin{align}
  B^*&=\max_{e,b}\frac{u_b^+}{\delta_{e,b}}, &
  D^*&=\max_{v,b}\frac{u_b^+}{\delta_{v,b}},\\
  A&=A_{\mathrm{ref}}, &
  B&=B^*+\epsilon,\qquad D=D^*+\epsilon,
  \label{eq:reachable-calibration}
\end{align}
where the maxima range over positive-demand pairs with defined
$\rho^*$; an empty maximum is zero.  Thus $B$ and $D$ remain global
edge and memory coefficients.

\paragraph{Guarantee.}
Since $A=A_{\mathrm{ref}}>P_0$, deleting the lower-utility of two bundles
selected for one request lowers $\calH$, so every ground state selects at
most one bundle per request; the competing load therefore lies in
$\mathcal R_{e,-r}$.  Now suppose a ground state overloads edge $e$ via a
selected $b\in B_r$, and let $\rho=\sum_{r'\neq r}d_{e,b_{r'}}$.  Then
$\rho\in\mathcal R_{e,-r}$ and $\rho+d_{e,b}>C_e$, so
$\rho\ge\rho^*_{e,b}$ and, by the monotonicity of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:drop-monotonicity}, $\Delta(\rho)\ge\delta_{e,b}$.
Removing $b$ then lowers the capacity term by at least
$B\delta_{e,b}>u_b^+$ while utility rises by at most $u_b^+$, and no
other term increases---contradicting minimality.  Memory is identical.
Ground states are thus feasible, all penalties vanish, and
$\calH=-\sum_b u_bx_b$: the ground states are exactly the maximum-utility
feasible selections.

\paragraph{Dominance.}
For integer demands and capacities, integer loads give
$\delta_{e,b},\delta_{v,b}\ge1$, so $u_b^+/\delta\le u_b^+\le P_0$ and
hence $B^*,D^*\le P_0$.  Both rules use the same $\epsilon$, so
$B\le B_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $D\le D_{\mathrm{ref}}$: each calibrated
capacity coefficient either decreases or remains equal to its
utility-scale reference value.

\subsection{Direct Slack-Free Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:direct}

The direct form removes the slack variables:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \calH_{\text{phys}}
  ={}&-\sum_{r,b}u_{r,b}x_{r,b}
  +\lambda_1\sum_r
    \sum_{\substack{b,b'\in B_r\\b<b'}}x_{r,b}x_{r,b'}\\
  &+\lambda_2\sum_e[L_e-C_e]_+^2
  +\lambda_3\sum_v[M_v-M_v^{\max}]_+^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:direct-ham}
\end{equation}
It uses exactly $\sum_r |B_r|$ binaries, eliminating the auxiliary
slacks, while the hinge terms leave under-capacity selections
unpenalized.

\subsection{Soft Congestion and Memory Risk}
\label{sec:congestion-risk}

Two physically motivated auxiliary terms shape the landscape:
\begin{align}
  \calH_{\text{cong}} &= \lambda_{\text{cong}} \sum_e
    \max\!\bigl(0,\, L_e/C_e - \theta\bigr)^2, \qquad
    \theta \in [0.5, 0.9],\\
  \calH_{\text{risk}} &= \lambda_{\text{risk}} \sum_{r,b}
    \Bigl(1 - e^{-\ell_{r,b}/\tau_{\text{mem}}}\Bigr)\, x_{r,b}.
\end{align}
$\calH_{\text{cong}}$ provides a smooth gradient toward load-balanced
configurations before hard capacity is reached; $\calH_{\text{risk}}$
penalizes bundles whose storage duration is comparable to the memory
coherence time $\tau_{\text{mem}}$.

\subsection{Chance-Constrained Fidelity Requirements}
\label{sec:chance-form}

The fidelity requirement $F_{r,b} \ge F_{\min}^{(r)}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:utility} treats the nominal (parametric) end-to-end
fidelity as if it were the delivered fidelity.  Under stochastic
delivery, the realized fidelity $F_r$ of request $r$ is random: in our
DES it is sampled from a truncated normal distribution centered at the
decayed nominal fidelity,
$F_r \sim \mathcal{TN}(F_{r,b}, \sigma^2; [0,1])$---a model of
measurement, gate, and readout noise of amplitude $\sigma$.  The
\emph{hard} rule
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:hard}
  F_{r,b} \ge F_{\min}^{(r)}
\end{equation}
therefore has an implicit, uncalibrated violation probability
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:pviol}
  p_{\text{viol}}(r,b) = \Pr[F_r < F_{\min}^{(r)}]
    = \Phi\!\left(\frac{F_{\min}^{(r)} - F_{r,b}}{\sigma}\right),
\end{equation}
which for a razor-margin bundle ($F_{r,b} \downarrow F_{\min}^{(r)}$)
approaches $1/2$ under symmetric noise: the deterministic rule can
tolerate up to half of deliveries violating the SLA.

The \emph{chance-constrained} rule replaces Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard} with
the quantile constraint
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:chance}
  \Pr[F_r \ge F_{\min}^{(r)}] \ge 1-\varepsilon
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  F_{r,b} \ge F_{\min}^{(r)} + \Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)\, \sigma,
\end{equation}
i.e. the requirement is moved up by a safety margin $z_{\varepsilon}
\sigma$ with $z_{\varepsilon} = \Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)$.  Because
$z_{\varepsilon} > 0$ for $\varepsilon < 1/2$, the chance constraint
is \emph{stricter} than the hard rule for every meaningful reliability
budget: it refuses bundles whose nominal fidelity clears the
threshold by less than the margin.  Only for $\varepsilon > 1/2$
(where $z_{\varepsilon} < 0$) does it admit near-miss bundles, trading
reliability for capacity.  The chance-constrained problem is solved
exactly as the hard problem---as a candidate-set filter
$\{b \in B_r : p_{\text{viol}}(r,b) \le \varepsilon\}$ followed by the
same selection ILP/QUBO---so all solvers of Sec.~\ref{sec:solvers}
apply unchanged.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:pviol} uses the exact truncated-normal
CDF, which differs from $\Phi$ only to order $e^{-\dots}$ in the tail
beyond $[0,1]$; for $\sigma \lesssim 0.05$ and $F \in [0.5,0.85]$ the
two agree to $10^{-6}$.

\section{Solvers and Scheduling Architectures}
\label{sec:solvers}

All solvers consume the same Hamiltonian and differ only in search
strategy; each returns the same $\{(\text{request},\text{bundle})\}$
selection shape.

\subsection{Metropolis Annealer}
\label{sec:metropolis}

The annealer explores configurations via local bit-flip moves, accepts
moves with probability $\min(1, e^{-\Delta \calH/T})$, and cools
geometrically from $T_0 = 10$ to $10^{-3}$
\cite{metropolis1953equation,rosales2025quantum}.  Because $\calH$ is a
sum of local and pairwise terms, $\Delta\calH$ is computed locally in
$\mathcal{O}(|\text{neighbors}|)$ time.  A greedy utility-density seed
accelerates convergence.  The same framework supports a
\emph{streaming} variant that re-optimizes the arriving request with a
short local sweep and periodically runs a full cooling cycle.

\subsection{Boundary-MPS Tensor-Network Compressor}
\label{sec:tn}

The tensor-network solver~\cite{rosales2025quantum,hao2022quantum,
nakada2025quick} represents the joint distribution over all requests'
bundle choices as a matrix product state.  Each request is a rank-3
tensor with a physical index over $B_r \cup \{\text{reject}\}$ and
virtual bonds carrying correlations to requests that share edges or
memory.  Left-to-right and right-to-left sweeps contract and truncate
the MPS to bond dimension $\chi$ via SVD
\cite{white1992dmrg,vidal2003efficient}; the dominant configuration is
decoded and any residual capacity violation repaired by greedy
descent.  A \emph{hard-constraint} variant
\cite{nakada2025quick} encodes capacity limits in the allowed index
configurations, removing the repair pass and the penalty
hyperparameters and guaranteeing feasibility by construction.

\subsection{OpenJij Simulated Annealing}
\label{sec:openjij}

We compile the penalty-based QUBO to a binary quadratic model and sample it with
OpenJij's simulated-annealing sampler~\cite{openjij}.  Penalty
coefficients $A,B,D$ are set either by the conventional utility-scale
rule or the proposed resource-aware rule (Sec.~\ref{sec:penalty-calibration});
the two are compared on held-out instances in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-penalty}.  OpenJij SA serves as the
reference against which the adaptive top-$k$ reduction
(Sec.~\ref{sec:adaptive-qubo}) is measured.

\subsection{Embedded Quantum-Annealing Backend}
\label{sec:qa}

For hardware-realistic annealing we minor-embed the QUBO onto a
Chimera-like hardware lattice with a greedy placement and inter-node
unit-weight chains, then sample with a path-integral quantum annealer
(PIA) sampler~\cite{openjij,venegas2018quantum}.  The decode is
chain-break-aware: broken chains are resolved by majority vote with a
local penalty check.  We report embedded qubit counts, chain-break
fractions, and solution quality versus SA and SQA
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-qa}).

\subsection{Temporal-Memory Joint Scheduling}
\label{sec:joint}

Static solvers assume all requests are known and resource demands are
static.  In practice requests arrive over time with deadlines, and
stored Bell pairs decay.  We add three components.

\subsubsection{Temporal requests.}
A \emph{temporal request} augments a request with an arrival time, a
hard deadline, a priority, and a slack
$\text{slack} = \text{deadline} - \text{arrival}$.  Dynamic traces are
generated as Poisson arrivals over $K$ slots with mean rate $\lambda$.

\subsubsection{Temporal memory scheduler.}
A stored pair's fidelity follows the T1/T2 law
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:decay}
  F_{\text{del}}(t) = F_0 \cdot \frac{1}{4}
    \Bigl[1 + 3 e^{-t/T_2}\,\frac{1 + e^{-t/T_1}}{2}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
which is the identity at $t=0$ and decays to the maximally mixed value
$1/4$.  The scheduler reserves memory \emph{windows}
$[\text{arrival},\text{completion}]$ against each node's capacity,
tracks per-pair fidelity via Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay}, and expires pairs
once $t$ exceeds the coherence time $\tau_{\text{mem}}$.

\subsubsection{Joint router.}
The joint scheduler assigns each arriving request a completion time and
a bundle, checking \emph{both} temporal-memory feasibility
(overlapping windows never exceed node capacity) and end-to-end
fidelity at the completion time, and is compared against a
memory-agnostic baseline that admits without tracking coherence.

\subsection{Adaptive QUBO Candidate Reduction}
\label{sec:adaptive-qubo}

Full QUBOs grow quadratically and anneal poorly at fixed budget.  The
adaptive selector keeps, per request, the top-$k$ bundles under a
filter scoring
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{r,b} = u_{r,b} \cdot \mathbf{1}[F_{r,b} \ge F_{\min}]
    - \lambda_{\text{cong}} \cdot \frac{\text{congested edges}}{|p_b|},
\end{equation}
discarding bundles that fail the fidelity threshold or that
disproportionately use congested edges; we sweep $k$ and compare
against the full reference QUBO under equal annealing effort.

\subsection{Hybrid Pipeline and GNN-Guided Ranking}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The hybrid pipeline chains \emph{reduce} (the filter of
Sec.~\ref{sec:adaptive-qubo}), \emph{solve} (anneal the reduced
QUBO), and \emph{repair/refine} (dominance-prune, greedy-repair any
residual violation, and re-seed a short anneal).  As a learned
alternative, a two-layer GraphSAGE encoder maps each bundle to
graph-structured features (memory load, capacity headroom, hop length,
path-average fidelity) and scores bundles; the top-$k$ scored bundles
feed a feasibility-aware greedy decoder
\cite{rusek2020routenets,mao2017routing}.  We compare full-QUBO,
filter-based top-$k$, and GNN-guided top-$k$ on identical instances.

\section{Stochastic Reliability Framework}
\label{sec:reliability}

\subsection{Discrete-Event Simulator}
\label{sec:des}

The DES samples the entanglement pipeline event by event: elementary
pair generation (with $p_{\text{gen}}$ per attempt, retries within
capacity), entanglement swapping (success $p_{\text{swap}}$),
purification, delivery under T1/T2 storage decay
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay}), and Gaussian fidelity noise of amplitude
$\sigma$ added at delivery.  For a fixed solver selection, it reports
the sampled expected utility $\E[U]$ (utility conditional on delivery),
the parametric--sampled \emph{reliability gap}, the served ratio, the
empirical SLA-violation probability against request thresholds, and
the per-delivery failure causes.  All noise is drawn from a seeded RNG
threaded through the engine, so runs are deterministic.

\subsection{Chance-Constrained Routing Study}
\label{sec:chance}

The study of Sec.~\ref{sec:chance-form} is executed as follows: for
each (request count $n$, noise $\sigma$), expand the candidate set;
solve the selection ILP exactly under each policy
(\texttt{hard}, \texttt{chance($\varepsilon$)} for
$\varepsilon \in \{0.05,\dots,0.8\}$, \texttt{nominal}); execute each
plan in the DES with matching $\sigma$; and report parametric utility,
sampled utility, served ratio, and empirical SLA violation.  The
reliability budget is tuned so that the DES realization is dominated
by the noise model (long $\tau_{\text{mem}}$), separating
noise-driven SLA risk from the decoherence-drift term, which is
studied separately by the temporal scheduler.

\subsection{Recourse and Optimality Certification}
\label{sec:recourse-opt}

\emph{Recourse.}  When a delivery fails, recourse either
\emph{repairs locally} (re-provisions the failed request with the best
bundles that still fit the residual capacities) or \emph{replans
fully} (re-runs the solver on all active requests).  We measure
recovery rate, recovered utility fraction, and wall-time speedup.
\emph{Optimality certification.}  For every benchmark instance we also
solve the selection ILP exactly (branch and bound) and report the
relative gap $(u^\ast - u_{\text{solver}})/u^\ast$ for each solver,
certifying how far heuristic annealing is from optimal.

\section{Analysis Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

\subsection{Purification as a First-Class Variable}
\label{sec:purification}

The core bundle generator exposes purification rounds $q \in \{0,1,2\}$
(and up to $q_{\max}=4$ in the purification-aware regime).  A
purification-aware candidate set includes, per request, bundles whose
fidelity \emph{requires} purification to meet $F_{\min}^{(r)}$; the
agnostic set restricts to $q=0$ (entanglement-only) plus the default
$q\in\{0,1,2\}$ set.  Because the candidate sets are supersets of the
agnostic set, the solver's optimum can only improve; we quantify when
purification actually binds via a fidelity sweep over path length and
$F_{\min}$.

\subsection{Multi-Objective and Disjoint-Path Analysis}
\label{sec:multiobj}

A selection can be scored by $\{t,f,s,\ell,m,c\}$ = \{throughput, mean
fidelity, success rate, latency, memory footprint, Bell-pair cost\}.
We enumerate all capacity-feasible selections and compute (i) the
\emph{Pareto frontier} under the maximize set $\{f,s,t\}$ and minimize
set $\{\ell,m,c\}$, and (ii) \emph{$\epsilon$-constraint frontiers}
(maximize $t$ subject to $f \ge \bar f$, and vice versa).  For requests
admitting $k$ edge-disjoint paths, a \emph{composite bundle} combines
subset $i$'s success probabilities as
$p_{\text{succ}} = 1 - \prod_i (1 - p_{\text{succ}}^{(i)})$ with
fidelity and latency equal to the constituent maxima; composite
bundles are Pareto-pruned and compared against single-path-only
provisioning.

\subsection{Swapping-Order Scheduling}
\label{sec:swapping}

A path of $L$ links can be fused by different \emph{swap trees};
because swapping is associative for Werner fidelities
($(f_1 \star f_2) \star f_3 = f_1 \star (f_2 \star f_3)$), the
noiseless end-to-end fidelity is identical for every tree.  Different
trees hold intermediate pairs in memory for different durations,
however: a \emph{linear} tree stores an intermediate pair for up to
$L-1$ swap rounds, while a \emph{balanced} tree of depth
$\lceil\log_2 L\rceil$ minimizes peak concurrency and maximizes
delivered (post-decay) fidelity for fixed $\tau_{\text{mem}}$.  We
sweep all Catalan-many trees and report linear, balanced, and optimal
delivered fidelity as a function of $L$ and $\tau_{\text{mem}}$.

\subsection{Generative Topologies, Time-Dependent Routing,
Q-Learning, Hardware Profiles}
\label{sec:extras}

We implement five generative families (ring, random-geometric,
Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi, Watts--Strogatz, Barab\'asi--Albert) on the
simulator's topology schema and sweep solver served ratio against
structural descriptors.  The time-dependent decoherence-aware router
aligns the risk term's hold model with the evaluation decay law
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay}) and scales the risk weight over the epoch.  A
tabular Q-learning router makes per-arrival bundle decisions from
quantized resource-pressure states~\cite{li2025qlearning,
boyan1993packet}.  Hardware profiles calibrate $T_1/T_2$, gate and
readout error rates, and per-anneal duration for ideal, photonic,
ion-trap, and superconducting platforms~\cite{lvovsky2009optical}.

\label{sec:results-penalty}

\subsection{Purification}
\label{sec:results-purification}

Table~\ref{tab:purification} and Figure~\ref{fig:purification} show
the purification comparison.  On the tested chains the solver selects
purification-free bundles in all three regimes (mean rounds
$k=0$), so a purification-aware candidate superset is \emph{costless}:
utility, served ratio, and fidelity are identical while the candidate
pool grows from 4 to 27 bundles ($N{=}6$).  The fidelity sweep
(Fig.~\ref{fig:purification}, right) shows when purification binds:
a single round lifts a 3-link path from $F=0.634$ to $F=0.703$,
meeting a $F_{\min}=0.7$ requirement that the $q{=}0$ bundle cannot
satisfy---at the price of success probability $0.145$ versus $0.512$
and doubled resource use.  Purification is therefore a Pareto
trade-off surface, not a free fidelity multiplier, and treating it as a
first-class optimization variable lets the solver price that trade-off
exactly.

\subsection{Prior-Extension Recap}
\label{sec:prior-wave}

Four extensions close our prior future-work list.
(i) A \emph{time-dependent decoherence-aware router} lifts delivered
fidelity from $0.188$ to $0.193$ ($+2.5\%$) on a Poisson trace at
equal served count by aligning the risk term's hold model with the
evaluation decay law.  (ii) A \emph{hard-constraint MPS} matches
penalty-MPS served ratio while being $1.4$--$1.7\times$ faster, with
feasibility guaranteed by construction.  (iii) A tabular
\emph{Q-learning router} matches the streaming annealer's utility
($189.5$, 6/7 served) with $0.2\,$ms inference versus $5.3\,$ms.
(iv) \emph{Hardware profiles}---ideal, photonic/fiber, ion-trap, and
superconducting---reveal a $2.8\times$ delivered-fidelity spread
($0.761$ down to $0.269$), quantifying when coherence-aware scheduling
is indispensable.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Deterministic fidelity constraints are an uncalibrated
reliability claim.}  The central methodological finding of this paper
is that the standard rule $F_{r,b} \ge F_{\min}^{(r)}$, ubiquitous in
entanglement-routing work, silently tolerates a violation probability
up to $1/2$ for any bundle whose nominal fidelity sits at the
threshold---and the empirical mean is $5$--$14\%$ even when the solver
selects high-quality plans.  The chance-constrained quantile rule
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:chance}) replaces this implicit, unmeasured risk with
an explicit, validated budget $\varepsilon$, and our DES study
confirms the empirical violation stays at or below $\varepsilon$.  We
recommend that routing papers report \emph{parametric and sampled
SLA jointly}, as we do in Table~\ref{tab:des}, so that throughput is
never reported without its fidelity cost.

\paragraph{The utility price of reliability is real but
controlled.}  Buying the guarantee from hard's implicit $\sim\!10\%$
down to $\varepsilon=0.05$ costs roughly $1.2$--$1.5\times$ in utility
because marginal (high-risk) bundles are refused.  This is the correct
economic statement: the operator trades capacity for certified
reliability with a single knob $\varepsilon$, and the frontier in
Fig.~\ref{fig:chance} (right) is the explicit menu.  Notably, the
chance-constrained set is no harder to optimize than the hard set
(mean QUBO gap $0.76$ on the reduced set), so the reliability
guarantee does not tax the solver.

\paragraph{Noise and decoherence are separable failure modes.}
The DES separates \emph{noise} (Gaussian fluctuations of delivered
fidelity) from \emph{decoherence} (deterministic decay during
storage).  Chance constraints calibrate the former; the temporal
scheduler (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}) and swap-tree ordering
(Sec.~\ref{sec:swapping}) address the latter; recourse
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-recourse}) mitigates residual stochastic
failure.  A plan that is optimal under one model need not be under the
other---the SQA plan's up-to-$8\%$ SLA violations (Table~\ref{tab:des})
are exactly the price of its $46\%$ mean optimality gap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:opt}).  Reliability analysis is therefore not a
post-processing nicety but a first-class objective.

\paragraph{Penalty tightening is regime-dependent.}
Reachable-load calibration changes the sufficient QUBO certificate,
not the constrained objective.  As request count grows, $\delta=1$
becomes more common and tightening rarer; this association is
consistent with the proposed mechanism but is not causal evidence.
Because every matched interval includes zero, the experiment neither
establishes solver equivalence nor detects systematic improvement under
the tested read budgets.  Fixing $A$ isolates capacity calibration;
joint scaling and hardware-range effects remain open.

\paragraph{Candidate reduction and purification are free until they
bind.}  Adaptive top-$k$, GNN-guided ranking, and purification-aware
candidate supersets all leave the reference optimum unchanged on our
benchmarks---reduction improves anneal quality at fixed budget, and
purification is never forced.  Each becomes binding in a defined
regime (budget $k=2$ on chains; $F_{\min}$ above the $q{=}0$ ceiling),
which the corresponding sweep quantifies.  This reframes both as
\emph{tunable reliability/capacity dials} rather than approximations.

\paragraph{Limitations.}  The MPS coupling order is heuristic; a
learned ordering could improve bond efficiency.  The GNN ranker is
trained per instance family and not yet shown to generalize across
topologies.  The joint scheduler assumes deterministic transmission;
stochastic success would require probabilistic windows.  The
truncated-normal noise model is a proxy for a full device-noise
characterization, and the chance-constraint calibration holds for the
modeled noise only---hardware validation on measured parameters
remains future work.  Our swap-tree analysis assumes independent T1/T2
decay without correlated dephasing.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a QUBO-based framework for entanglement routing whose
solver stack (Metropolis, boundary-MPS, OpenJij SA, embedded QA) is
certified against exact ILP and extended from static, memory-blind
routing toward the stochastic, temporal, and learned regime that
deployed quantum networks will require.  The key findings are:
(i) solver families agree on the capacity-limited served-ratio
frontier, with the MPS solver $5$--$4{,}000\times$
faster and Metropolis within $1.5\%$ of the exact ILP; (ii) the
direct slack-free Hamiltonian dominates the slack-variable QUBO in
size and quality, while the request-conditioned reachable-load
calibration preserves exact ground-state correspondence, tightens at
least one capacity coefficient in $12.9\%$ of benchmark instances, and
yields no detected systematic SA/SQA difference under the tested read
budget;
(iii) temporal-memory joint scheduling delivers
every admitted request within coherence at fidelity $0.30$--$0.51$,
where a memory-agnostic baseline over-admits; (iv) chance-constrained
routing turns the implicit $5$--$14\%$ SLA risk of the deterministic
rule into a validated, tunable $\varepsilon$, with the empirical
violation at or below budget on every tested instance; (v) recourse
recovers $100\%$ of failures at $334$--$1065\times$ speedup; (vi)
candidate reduction, purification, and $k$-disjoint provisioning are
free-or-beneficial until defined regimes make them bind; (vii)
balanced swap trees recover up to $1.58\times$ of delivered fidelity
under memory decay; (viii) solver quality survives across five
topology families, degrading gracefully only on scale-free networks.

Remaining future work includes: adaptive bond dimension driven by
truncation error; function-approximation RL that generalizes across
topologies; learned MPS coupling order; stochastic-window joint
scheduling; hardware validation of the noise model on measured testbed
parameters; and chance-constrained joint scheduling that co-optimizes
$\varepsilon$ with the temporal admission decision.
